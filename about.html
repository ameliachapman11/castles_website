About the book

Concept

Castles in the Air is an introduction to the world of mathematical logic cast in the form of a fantasy novel — set in a universe where numbers, sets, theorems, etc. are manifested as physical objects. I conceived the idea around 1998, and the writing has occupied me intermittently since 2009. Besides seeking to make some difficult mathematical concepts accessible at an intuitive level, the book explores a wide range of philosophical viewpoints and the debates they give rise to (without settling on any final conclusions).

The bulk of the book (406 pages) is occupied by the main story which may be read as a novel. An appendix (124 pages) offers commentaries that expand on the ideas within each chapter and provide some historical background.

While presupposing no more than basic school-level maths, the book takes the reader into some advanced territory and tackles complex and mind-stretching ideas — so be prepared for a challenging intellectual ride!

About the author

I am a lecturer in the School of Informatics, University of Edinburgh, and an active researcher in mathematical logic and the theory of programming languages.

My monograph, Higher-Order Computability, co-authored with Dag Normann, was published by Springer in 2015.
